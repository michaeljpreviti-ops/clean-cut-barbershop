Accessibility at Clean Cut

We want anyone who needs a haircut to be able to use this website — including people who browse with a screen reader, navigate by keyboard, enlarge text, or need strong color contrast. This page sets out exactly what we have done, what we tested, and what we know is still imperfect.

The standard we follow

The Americans with Disabilities Act does not itself contain a technical checklist for websites. The U.S. Department of Justice's guidance on web accessibility and the ADA points to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) as the recognized measure, and in April 2024 the Department adopted WCAG 2.1 Level AA as the binding standard for state and local government websites.

Clean Cut is a private business, so that government rule is not directly binding on us. We have adopted WCAG 2.1 Level AA anyway, because it is the benchmark courts and the Department use when they assess whether a business is offering its goods and services on an equal basis.

Going down the ada.gov list

The Department's guidance names six common barriers. Here is each one, in its own words, and what we did about it.

  • ✓
    “Poor color contrast” Every piece of text on the site was measured against the color behind it. All of it now meets or beats the 4.5:1 ratio AA requires for normal text, and 3:1 for large text. The headline over the shop photograph was measured pixel-by-pixel against the actual image — it reaches 8:1, and the smallest text over that photo reaches 5.6:1.
  • ✓
    “Use of color alone to give information” Nothing on this site is communicated by color on its own. When you pick a service or a time, the choice is shown by a tick appearing in a box — a change of shape, not just a change of color. Today's row in the opening hours is marked with bold text as well as a dot. Form problems are always explained in words, never signaled by a red border alone.
  • ✓
    “Lack of text alternatives (‘alt text’) on images” The photograph of the shop carries a short written description. The barber-pole logo and the decorative pattern are marked so screen readers skip them instead of reading out noise.
  • –
    “No captions on videos” Not applicable — there is no video or audio anywhere on this site. If we ever add any, it will ship with accurate captions that identify who is speaking.
  • ✓
    “Inaccessible online forms” Every field in the booking form has a real label attached to it, is grouped under a heading that explains what the group is for, and fills in from your device's saved contact details. If something is wrong, the form says what is wrong and how to fix it in plain words — “Enter a 10-digit mobile number so we can confirm” — and announces that message to screen readers rather than only showing it.
  • ✓
    “Mouse-only navigation (lack of keyboard navigation)” The whole site, booking included, can be operated from the keyboard alone. Every control shows a clear gold outline when it has focus, a “skip to main content” link lets you jump past the header, and the three booking steps are real buttons you can reach with Tab and activate with Enter or Space.

The guidance makes four further recommendations, which we have also followed: text resizes and the page zooms without breaking, the page is built out of proper headings so a screen reader can jump between sections, the layout reflows down to a 320-pixel-wide screen without side-to-side scrolling, and — as set out at the bottom of this page — there is a way to tell us when something is wrong.

How this was tested

The Department's guidance warns that automated checkers alone are not enough, and that a clean report does not prove a site is accessible. So the site was checked three ways:

  • WAVE (WebAIM's evaluation tool) — reported 0 errors.
  • axe-core 4.10.2, run against WCAG 2.0 A/AA, 2.1 A/AA, 2.2 AA and best-practice rules, on the page at rest and again with the booking form filled in and submitted — 0 violations, 48 checks passing.
  • Checked by hand — contrast ratios calculated directly from the color values, including sampling the actual pixels of the shop photograph behind the headline; the booking flow completed using only the keyboard; heading structure, landmarks and focus order reviewed; layout tested at 320, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 pixels wide, and again with text spacing increased.

Eight issues were found and fixed in that process, including four contrast failures, selection states that relied on color alone, booking-step headings that could not be reached by keyboard, and a header that forced sideways scrolling on a narrow phone.

What we know is still imperfect

We would rather say this plainly than claim perfection:

  • No one has tested this site with an actual screen reader yet. The structure, labels and announcements were verified in code and with automated tools, but that is not the same as a person using JAWS, NVDA or VoiceOver end to end. That testing is the clear next step.
  • No disabled users have reviewed it. Automated tools and a careful developer catch a great deal, but not everything that makes a site genuinely usable.
  • The booking form needs JavaScript. If JavaScript is switched off or fails to load, the form is replaced by a message giving the shop's phone number and address instead.
  • The stripes on the barber-pole logo turn continuously. They carry no information, and they hold still for anyone whose device asks for reduced motion, but there is no on-page button to stop them.
  • One link is smaller than the 24-pixel touch target WCAG 2.2 suggests — the phone number inside the sentence in the footer. It qualifies for that rule's exception for links inside a line of text, and the same number appears as a large button elsewhere on every page.

If any part of this website gets in your way — or if you need to book a cut in a different way — contact the shop and we will sort it out and fix the site. Tell us the page you were on and what went wrong, and we will reply as quickly as we can.

Technical details

This site is built as plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework. It relies on standard, well-supported web features and is designed to work in current versions of Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge, on desktop and on phones. It does not use an accessibility overlay or widget — the Department's guidance cautions against treating those as a fix, so the accessibility work is built into the page itself.
